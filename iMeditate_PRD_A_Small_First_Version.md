**PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS**

**iMeditate**

Version 1: The Small First Version

**Product:**  iMeditate, a mobile app for meditating on scripture

**Audience:**  Children ages 4 to 17, their parents, and any adult

**Platforms:**  Mobile phones and tablets

**Purpose:**  Prove the heart of the app with real families before building more

# **1\. Overview**

iMeditate helps children, and anyone, meditate on scripture using the three steps drawn from the Hebrew word hagar: Ponder, Mutter, and Roar. A child sees a scripture, pictures themselves inside it, says it quietly and repeatedly, and then declares it aloud with confidence and joy. Along the way, a Soldier companion grows in rank as the child completes more scriptures.

This document describes Version 1, the smallest version worth putting in front of real families. It includes only the essentials. Everything else in the full plan is deliberately left out so we can learn quickly whether the core experience moves children.

| The one thing Version 1 must prove A child can complete the three steps on a scripture, enjoy it, want to come back, and feel proud watching their Soldier grow. |
| :---- |

# **2\. Goals and What Success Looks Like**

## **Goals**

* Give a child a complete, satisfying meditation from start to finish using the three steps.

* Make the Soldier a companion the child cares about and wants to see grow.

* Give a parent a safe, simple way to set up their child.

* Learn what works from a small group of real families before investing in more.

## **How we will know it is working**

| Question | What a good answer looks like |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Do children finish a scripture?** | Most children who start a scripture reach Roar and finish it. |
| **Do children come back?** | A meaningful share of children return on a second and third day. |
| **Do children care about the Soldier?** | Children ask about the Soldier, talk about his rank, and are excited when he is promoted. |
| **Do parents feel it is safe and clear?** | Parents finish setting up without help and say they would let their child use it alone. |
| **Is it understandable at every age?** | Both a young child and a teenager can follow it without an adult explaining. |

# **3\. Who It Is For**

# **5\. The First-Time Journey**

1. **Open and sign up as a parent.** Version 1 is parent-led: the adult creates an account with Email + Password. Meditating for oneself without a child waits for a later version.

2. **Verify the email.** A 6-digit code confirms the inbox (15 minutes). Nothing else unlocks until this is done.

3. **Welcome and PIN.** A welcome message explains what happens next, and the parent sets the PIN that guards the parent area.

4. **Add the children.** The parent chooses how many children (up to five) and adds each with only a first name or nickname and an age group. No photo, location, or school details are ever asked for.

4. **Meet the Soldier.** Each child meets their own Soldier at his first rank and sees a short, friendly introduction to the three steps.

5. **Arrive at home.** The home screen shows the Soldier, today's suggested scripture with a big Start button, and a way to browse the theme.

6. **Do the three steps.** The child completes Ponder, Mutter, and Roar for the scripture.

7. **Celebrate.** If the Soldier is promoted, the child sees a short rank-up celebration. Otherwise they see a warm message from the Soldier.

# **6\. The Three Steps**

Each step grows out of the one before it. A step unlocks only when the earlier step is complete for that scripture.

## **Step 1: Ponder**

Ponder means having a deep, personal understanding of the scripture, picturing how it applies to you, and imagining yourself immersed in it.

1. The scripture is read aloud.

2. Guiding questions appear one at a time, such as "Where are you in this scene?" and "What do you see, hear, and feel?"

3. The child writes or draws what they imagined. It is saved.

4. There is no timer. The child taps "I'm done" when ready.

## **Step 2: Mutter**

Mutter means speaking the scripture under your breath, again and again.

* The scripture appears one phrase at a time.

* The child says each phrase quietly.

* A counter tracks the repetitions. The goal is 20 times.

* Soft background sound plays throughout.

* The app does not use the microphone. Nothing is recorded.

## **Step 3: Roar**

Roar means declaring the scripture loudly, with confidence, excitement, and joy.

* Uplifting music plays while the child declares the scripture.

* The words light up as the child goes.

* The mood here is the boldest and most energetic in the whole app.

## **The focus rule**

Focus is part of the practice. If a child leaves for more than 5 minutes, they restart from Ponder. To keep this fair:

* The Soldier gives a gentle warning before the 5 minutes run out.

* Interruptions such as a phone call pause the clock.

* Anything the child wrote or drew in Ponder is kept, even after a restart.

# **7\. Wording by Age Group**

Because the app serves ages 4 to 17, the parent chooses an age group and the app adjusts its wording, guiding questions, and the look of the Soldier.

| Age group | How the app feels |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Ages 4 to 7** | Very simple words. Everything is read aloud. A playful Soldier. |
| **Ages 8 to 11** | Simple wording. Reading and listening are both offered. |
| **Ages 12 to 17** | More grown-up wording. A heroic Soldier and a story that does not feel like a cartoon. |

# **8\. The Soldier**

The Soldier is the child's companion and the face of the brand. He stays with the child through all three steps and reacts to their progress.

* He starts at the first rank, Recruit.

* His rank rises when a child completes new scriptures. In Version 1 he can rise through the first few ranks (Recruit, Private, Sergeant, and Captain) using the quick early pace.

* Armies gather under him as he rises.

* The tone is always adventurous and joyful, never violent.

| A note on the free content Version 1 has only 3 scriptures. At the quick early pace, a child who finishes all 3 will likely see the Soldier promoted at least once. That is the moment we want the first experience to end on. |
| :---- |

Later ranks (General, the stars, and ruling cities, continents, and regions) are part of the full plan and will need their own gentle pacing.

# **9\. The Parent's Role in Version 1**

* Creates the account, verifies the email, and adds up to five children.

* Sees a simple confirmation that the child is set up.

* Is protected by a simple PIN if they enter the parent area, so a child cannot wander in.

* Can delete the child's profile and all of its information at any time.

The full progress view (streaks, time spent, and the step reached on each scripture) is not in Version 1\. Parents may also meditate for themselves in a simple way, using the same Sound Mind theme, but a separate personal plan waits for the full plan.

# **10\. Look and Feel**

* Bold and adventurous, with deep blues and reds and lots of energy.

* The Soldier is part of the brand, including the app icon.

* The mood shifts with the step: quieter and steadier on the home screen, Ponder, and Mutter, then bolder and livelier for Roar and the rank-up celebration.

# **11\. Safety and Trust**

Because children use this app, trust comes first. These promises apply from day one.

* No ads, ever.

* No chatting or messaging between users.

* Minimal information collected: a first name or nickname and an age group only, with no photos, location, or school details.

* The microphone is never used.

* Everything is stored privately, and a parent can delete a child's profile and all of its information at any time.

* Children never see prices or buy buttons.

# **12\. Scripture Content**

Version 1 includes one theme, Sound Mind, with 3 scriptures. Each scripture needs the following prepared before launch:

* The scripture text, with clear phrase breaks for Mutter.

* An audio reading for Ponder.

* Guiding questions written for each of the three age groups.

* Soft background sound for Mutter and uplifting music for Roar.

# **13\. Risks and Open Questions**

| Risk or question | What we plan to do |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Children may find 20 repetitions of Mutter long.** | Watch closely in early testing. Adjust the phrase-by-phrase pacing before changing the number. |
| **The 5-minute rule may feel harsh to some children.** | Test the gentle warning. Confirm that kept Ponder work makes restarts feel fair. |
| **The wide age range (4 to 17\) is hard to serve with one design.** | Test with at least one family in each age group, and refine the wording for each. |
| **The Soldier might not appeal to every child.** | Ask children directly. Consider a companion choice in a later version. |
| **Bible translation permissions.** | Choose a translation and confirm its terms before content is prepared. |
| **Permission to use Pastor Chris Oyakhilome's messages.** | Not needed for Version 1\. Begin the permission request now so it is ready for later versions. |

# **15\. Technical Stack and Why It Was Chosen**

These choices serve the PRD's constraints: real-family testing on phones and tablets, children's safety first, minimal spend, and a V1 architecture that never needs a rewrite when the full plan arrives.

| Choice | What it is | Why |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Framework: Expo React Native + TypeScript** | One codebase for iOS and Android, phones and tablets | Reaches every test family from a single build; offline and audio support for the three steps; store releases for testing |
| **Database: Postgres on our own device (Docker)** | Full SQL database running locally, not in a cloud | Zero hosting cost; holds the entire schema (V1 plus jewels, streaks, entitlements) from day one; children's data stays on our machine, the strongest safety posture |
| **Authentication: Better Auth (Email + Password)** | Self-hosted sign-in against our own database, parent accounts only | No auth vendor and no per-user fees; matches the onboarding design (Email + Password); children exist only as profiles under a parent, so a child can never hold credentials |
| **File storage: Cloudflare R2** | The only cloud piece; holds audio, artwork, and Ponder uploads | Generous free tier with zero egress fees, which matters when Roar audio and message clips stream later; presigned URLs keep files private |
| **Local hosting for now** | API and database run on our device; the phone connects over WiFi | Nothing to pay or configure in clouds during V1; same schema and storage move to hosted infrastructure later without app changes |
| **No Supabase, no Vercel** | Deliberately excluded | Keeps the stack free, self-contained, and under our control while testing with families |

Every other service follows the same free-first rule: our own analytics table instead of a vendor, free crash reporting, and store releases only — so Version 1 needs no paid service at all.

## **16\.5\. Components and principles**

Buttons: Ember Amber for every primary action, Ghost for secondary. Mutter progress is a row of dots — filled dot per repetition, never a numeric-only counter. Rank badge and streak chip carry earned status. The focus warning reads "Your Soldier is waiting — come back in the next minute to keep your place."

1. **Earned colour stays earned.** Gold appears only at real achievement, or the reward stops meaning anything.
2. **The palette narrows as focus deepens.** Only Roar goes loud.
3. **The Soldier is illustrated, not iconographic.** A character, not a logo; the age shift lives in his art while the UI stays age-agnostic.
4. **Scripture text is never decorated.** The one visual constant a 5-year-old and a 17-year-old both read the same way.
